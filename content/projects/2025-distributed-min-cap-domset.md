description: "The study of dominating sets lies at the heart of algorithmic graph theory, connecting structural graph properties with optimization and decision-making in networked systems. In its classical form, the Minimum Dominating Set (MDS) problem models the selection of a minimal subset of vertices that collectively cover the entire graph. This abstraction underpins numerous applications, including network routing, facility placement, and sensor coverage. However, the assumption that a dominator can serve an unbounded number of neighbors is rarely valid in modern distributed systems. In cloud infrastructures, for instance, computational and communication resources are limited. In IoT networks, nodes are constrained by power or connectivity range. These resource limitations motivate the Capacitated Dominating Set (CDS) problem, which augments MDS with capacity constraints that bound how many neighbors each dominator can cover. The capacitated formulation more accurately captures the operational realities of load distribution, service assignment, and resource management in heterogeneous distributed systems. From a theoretical standpoint, capacitated domination represents a rich and challenging generalization of classical domination. It combines elements of combinatorial optimization, facility location, and load balancing, and it exhibits substantially different complexity behavior. Even under structural restrictions such as bounded-degree or planar graphs, the capacitated problem remains NP-hard and often intractable under standard parameterizations. Yet, it holds deep connections to key algorithmic themes in graph theory, parameterized complexity, approximation schemes, and distributed local algorithms."
